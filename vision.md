# Vision

## Product Thesis

Daily art should teach people how to look, not just give them text to read.

Most art apps treat the painting as an illustration for an article. We want the painting to stay central throughout the experience so the user learns by looking closely, noticing details, and forming a personal response. The goal is not simply to deliver art history facts. The goal is to change how someone sees a familiar work.

## The User Problem

People on their commute often want to learn something meaningful in a short burst, usually around five minutes, and they may not have a reliable internet connection. Existing art apps prove there is demand for daily art discovery, but they often feel text-heavy, visually flat, or too broad. They tell users about art instead of guiding them through it.

We want to create a lightweight daily ritual that feels calm, beautiful, and rewarding even in a weak-signal environment.

## Core Promise

Each day, the user gets one famous painting, one surprising insight, and one short guided visual journey.

In five minutes, the user should come away with:
- a quick understanding of who made the work and why it matters
- a new way of seeing a specific detail or area in the painting
- a stronger personal opinion about the work itself

## Product Pillars

### Beautiful and Editorial

The product should feel like a premium piece of editorial design rather than a content feed. The painting is the hero. Typography, spacing, motion, and layout should direct attention toward the artwork and give the lesson room to breathe.

### Guided-Looking, Not Text-Dump

The experience should actively guide the eye through the work. Each lesson should point to specific areas of the painting and explain what to notice there. Reading and looking should happen together.

### Short, Specific, Memorable

Lessons should fit naturally into a short commute. They must be clear, human-readable, and tightly edited. Users should remember one or two specific observations, not feel like they skimmed a museum label.

### Offline-Friendly

The product should be dependable when users lose signal underground or in transit. Once connected, they should have the current lesson and a short forward cache available without extra effort.

### Generous and Low-Friction

The early product should feel welcoming. No forced accounts, no aggressive paywalls, no interruptions that break the calm of the experience. We should earn the right to monetize later.

## V1 Experience

V1 is intentionally narrow and focused:
- one daily painting for everyone
- the painting remains visible throughout the lesson
- the lesson unfolds as a scroll-led narrative
- the structure is brief setup, guided visual beats, then a short reflection

The first version should feel complete even though it is small. It should prove that guided-looking can make famous works feel fresh again.

## Content Principle

We should start with famous works first.

Recognisable paintings give the product an immediate hook. The user should feel that even if they already know the artwork, they are being shown something they have never properly noticed before.

Every lesson must include at least one non-obvious observation that changes how the user sees the painting. A useful example is not simply saying that the Mona Lisa is famous, but guiding attention toward how her gaze or expression can be read in a more ambiguous, even melancholic, way when the user looks carefully.

AI can generate first-pass lessons, but the format must force specificity and readability. The product should reject generic summaries, vague praise, or art-school cliches. The writing must always point to something visible and concrete in the painting.

## Differentiation

We are not trying to build a broader encyclopedia first. We are trying to build a better daily experience.

The product should be:
- more beautiful than DailyArt
- more visually guided than current art-learning apps
- more humane and less pushy than subscription-heavy competitors

The wedge is not just "daily art," but "daily guided looking."

## Long-Term Direction

If the core experience works, the product can grow into a larger art-learning platform.

Natural expansion paths include:
- broader canon and stronger global representation
- richer archive browsing
- favorites and personal collections
- curated learning paths
- widgets and notification controls
- native iOS and later Android store apps from the same shared foundation

But the first job is simpler: prove that one daily painting, presented beautifully and taught through close looking, can become a habit.
